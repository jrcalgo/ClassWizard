PPOL 8640 - Economic Analysis of Public Policy, PPOL 8640 Section number: 090 Cross listed courses: PPOL 8640-090 is cross-listed with ECON 6090-090 Co-requisites: none Pre-requisites: none; enrollment is limited to students admitted to the Ph.D. in Public Policy program or by permission of the instructor Student Learning outcomes: This is a core course in the Ph.D. in Public Policy program. The course is designed to help students define and explain the fundamental concepts of economic theory and the assumptions required for their predictions to hold. Over the course of the semester, students apply models of consumer choice, profit maximization, and market equilibrium to policy settings, and identify the conditions under which markets succeed and fail. At the conclusion, students will be able to predict and evaluate the outcomes that economic theory implies for policy tools such as taxes, price controls, tariffs and quotas, antitrust regulation, and behavioral interventions. Course Description | Economics is a tool used to impart a logically consistent view of a complex society. This course expands students' toolkits through a careful understanding of fundamental economic concepts and how these concepts can be used to predict the outcomes of policies. The class is not focused on making normative judgments about different policies, but instead on what tradeoffs economic theory would predict.
